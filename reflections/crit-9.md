# Crit 9 reflection

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

The breakthrough came when I narrowed three ideas i had into one. I had explored a shared preparedness checklist that synced tasks like “secure outdoor items” or “fuel the car” based on active warnings, and a community layer where users confirm conditions inside a warning area, such as flash flooding on a street. Both had value, but I realised the simplest idea was the strongest: tapping the map to drop a weather report that appears instantly as an icon for everyone viewing that area, then fades after an hour or two. It needs no setup, no group and no explanation, so anyone can contribute in seconds. Every report adds local detail the radar alone can’t show, and each new user makes the map more useful for the next. That shift from building features to building a community of contributors is what moved the work forward.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?**
I think this crit taught me that the best features are often the ones that let users help each other. Weighing the community pin against a shared preparedness checklist or a confirmed-conditions layer, I first thought it might be better to favour the more complex ideas because they felt bigger. The pin won because it was the easiest to use and invited everyone to take part. That changed how I judge my work. I want to be a developer who helps user contribute quickly and foster user collaboration. I also learned that collaborative features carry responsibility. If strangers rely on each other’s reports during a storm, I need to design for trust from the start, with clear labels, report expiry and moderation. Going forward, I want to develop features that makes people feel like they're a part of something, keeps them safe and grows more valuable as more people use it.
